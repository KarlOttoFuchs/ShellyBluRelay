Rev B Enclosure Concept

ESP32-C3 LED controller, inline tube enclosure · concept sketch, 2026-10-01 · model: enclosure_rev_b.py (FreeCAD 1.1, parametric) · all dimensions mm

An inline stick that sits in the 24 V cable run between the always-on PSU at the back of the counter and the COB strip at the front lip. The board slides into a printed tube on rails; two end caps carry the cable entry, strain relief and a centred screw tab, and click onto the tube with snap detents. No screws or inserts: once mounted, the two tab screws fix the caps to the cabinet and the tube is trapped between them. Assembly order

  1. Thread the strip cable through CAP OUT, then through the tube from the OUT end.
  2. Thread the PSU cable through CAP IN. Strip both jackets ~10 mm inside where each cap chamber will be, and fit a cable tie tight on each jacket as the tie-stop.
  3. Push the conductors into both connectors with the board outside the IN end.
  4. Slide the board into the tube from the IN end, drawing the strip cable back.
  5. Push both caps on until they click (tube ends against the stop ribs).
  6. Hold each cable aside, drive a countersunk wood screw through each end tab, then let the cable lie back over the screw head. Antenna (+Y) side facing open space. The two wood screws now hold everything; the detents only matter off the cabinet.

Bench access: pull CAP IN off by hand and slide the board out; either release the OUT wires or leave enough slack to pull the board clear with the strip cable still attached.

Printing

  • PETG, not PLA (kitchen heat). 0.2 mm layers, 3 perimeters so the detent grooves keep a solid wall behind them.
  • Tube: print upright (axis vertical). Rails and hold-down ribs grow straight off the walls, so no supports.
  • Caps (2 × the same STL): print on the mounting face, tab flat on the bed, so the tab layers run along its length and the countersink prints upright. The cap roof bridges ~25 mm; print a bridge test first if unsure of your PETG settings.
  • LED window: leave open, fill with clear resin, or print the tube in natural PETG and reduce the window to a 0.6 mm skin.
